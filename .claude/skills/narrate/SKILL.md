# Narrate

Turning a beat outline into story prose. A story is **lived in scenes**
and **built to one moment of change**; the reader feels the change by
contrast with where it began.

Stories written here only. Everything else on this blog is technical
writing.

## The test

For any passage: is the reader *watching something happen*, or *being
told a conclusion*? Watching is story; told is report. Turn the told
passages into scenes, or cut them.

## Principles

Each names what to do and the criterion that confirms it.

- **Build to one moment of change.** Name the single thing that shifted;
  every scene aims at it. *Confirmed when:* you can state the moment in
  one sentence, and only one.
- **Open at its opposite.** Start the prose furthest from the landing,
  so the change registers as contrast. *Confirmed when:* the first scene
  and the last are recognisably opposite states.
- **Write scenes, not summary.** Put the reader in a concrete moment
  with documentary detail — real dates, names, the thing that actually
  happened. *Confirmed when:* the passage shows something a reader could
  picture, not a span of time narrated from above.
- **Past tense for past action.** *Confirmed when:* present tense
  appears only for what is true now, never as faux-immediacy for a past
  event.
- **Braid fact and feeling.** Every beat carries something concrete and
  something felt. *Confirmed when:* no scene is a fact-dump (a tooling
  list with no interior) and none is sentiment with nothing to anchor
  it.
- **Plain diction.** Say the plain word. *Confirmed when:* the prose
  names things the way you would aloud; craft vocabulary ("pull",
  "weight", "braid") stays in the workshop.
- **Causal narrative.** Show the mechanism — "Without X, Y happens."
  *Confirmed when:* a reader learns *how* one thing led to another, not
  just that two things differ.
- **Observe, don't apologise.** Retrospect owns what happened ("I did
  X") without regret ("I should have"). *Confirmed when:* the
  looking-back reads as account, not confession.
- **Land on substance.** Close on a real observation or honest limit.
  *Confirmed when:* the last line says something rather than restating
  the post or drumrolling toward it.

## Procedure

1. Take the confirmed outline; write each scene from its beats.
2. Keep the braid live beat by beat — concrete and felt together.
3. Run the test on every paragraph: watching, or told? Fix the told
   ones.
4. Hand to the voice pass (draft-post §4) for sentence-level conventions
   — grammar lean, possessives, dashes.

Provenance: Matthew Dicks, *Storyworthy* (moment of change, start at its
opposite); Jack Hart, *Storycraft* (scene, arc, story-versus-report);
Lorin Hochstein, "The Power of Stories" (SREcon Americas 2026, the
fact/feeling braid).
