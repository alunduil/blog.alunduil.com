I went straight from [*Orconomics*][orconomics] into
[*Son of a Liche*][liche], the second book of J. Zachary Pike's Dark Profit
Saga. It picks up a year later, with Gorm Ingerson's party living as outlaws
while they try to make amends to the orcs they betrayed. Each of them is
keeping a secret from the others, which is what splits the party up. I think
it's an excellent continuation of the first book. Each of the heroes grows
in confidence as the book goes.

Gorm drives most of that growth. In *Orconomics* his loyalty to a goblin
squire and a band of misfits was a lot of why he was so lovable. In this book
he puts it to work.
<!-- vale blog-alunduil-com.Spelling = NO -->
"I ain't the sort for quittin',"
<!-- vale blog-alunduil-com.Spelling = YES -->
he tells the first of his old companions he goes after. "And I hang on to
things once I've made up my mind." He brings each member of the party back in
turn. By the time they're together again they act like a group of heroes, and
a surer one than before.

The party's two mages learn to fight together. In one brawl Jynn keeps trying
out new weaves while Laruna tells him to stop experimenting and fight. One of
his small spells glances off an enemy's shield into her fireball and splits
it. Half of the fire rains down on the enemy's head. "You messed up my spell,"
Laruna says, and Pike writes her as more shocked than angry. The scene plays
for a laugh while the two of them work out how to help each other.

Early on, a banker tells the king that investors treat shares of the dragon's
hoard
<!-- vale proselint.Cliches = NO -->
"as good as gold."
<!-- vale proselint.Cliches = YES -->
I read the dragon as standing, literally and figuratively, for what backs
Arth's economy. I don't know yet where [*Dragonfired*][dragonfired] takes
that.
