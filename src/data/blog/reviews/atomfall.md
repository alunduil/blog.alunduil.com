Somewhere on a hillside in the Windscale quarantine zone there is a
cast-iron bathtub on cart wheels. Three skeletons are sitting up in it. One
wears a flat cap. Another has a knitted beanie pulled down over the skull.
Someone dressed them, propped them upright, and pushed the tub out into the
grass. [*Atomfall*](https://store.steampowered.com/app/801800/Atomfall/)
never tells you who, or why. That's the reason I'd tell you to play it.

![Three skeletons propped upright in a cast-iron bathtub mounted on cart wheels, parked on a steep grassy hillside under a clear sky. One wears a flat cap, another a knitted beanie.](/assets/atomfall-bathtub.jpg)

There are no quest markers. You get leads instead—a note in a drawer, a set
of coordinates, a name someone lets slip. You place your own icons on the
map from what they imply. Progress depends on working out where to go next,
not on walking toward a marker the game has already lit for you. I spent
much of the eight and a half hours wrong-footed. That turned out to be the
point rather than the cost.

Fallout is the comparison everyone reaches for. It's also the right one.
*Atomfall* works the same
post-apocalyptic ground and arrives somewhere else on it. The zone is bright
green under a blue sky, all wet grass and birch scrub and wildflowers. The
jokes are English. None of it reads as a wasteland, which is what keeps you
curious.

The constraints are what most people complain about. They're the best thing
here. You carry four guns and sixteen other items. There's no money anywhere
in the zone, so trading means piling goods onto a set of scales until the
merchant's side balances. Reviewers named the inventory the game's worst
decision. I think they have it backwards. The limit is what stops the zone
becoming a supply run. Bach
set himself all twenty-four keys and wrote the *Well-Tempered Clavier*
inside that rule. The constraint is where the invention came from.

Red Strain and Wicked Isle give you two more mysteries rather than more
zone.

Breadth is where it goes wrong. Follow every side story and they still
converge on one decision, so six mutually exclusive endings collapse into
whichever you took. The game's answer is a manual save beforehand and a
reload after—an odd ask from something that otherwise trusts you to work
everything out unaided.

I don't know how much of my defence of that inventory is the inventory and
how much is the length. Eight and a half hours is short. A limit that forces
good choices over eight hours might be plain friction over forty. I never
played the version where that gets tested.
