I'd assumed dyeing was steam in a pot and done. I backed Caitlin ffrench's
[*Gathering Colour*][book] on [Kickstarter][kickstarter] to learn how it
works and how to work with colours. It has some good instruction, but also
more ritual and belief than I wanted and a lot of dry repetition. Unless
you're big into crafting with your own supplies, I don't think it's worth the
time.

The paint chapters gave me the most to latch onto, since I studied physics
and have dabbled a little in painting. Paint is pigment in a binder, about
three parts to one, and sits on top of the paper where ink soaks in. I had
nothing like that to build on for dyeing. The book shows that fibre has to be
scoured and then mordanted before any dye will hold. I appreciate the process
a lot more now. I never knew how these materials went together or how to
apply them. Now I have a reference if I need it.

The first and last parts of the book centre on ritual and belief. The first
covers the eight pagan holidays and the phases of the moon. The last covers
moon water, incense, and the four elements. Projects in the middle, like the
eco-printed patch and the altar cloth, open with the magic in them before the
materials list. The rule against gathering flowers after the Harvest Moon
comes up four times. That's the author's prerogative. I wanted the
instruction.

The repetition makes it dry. The solstice wreath appears as a ritual near the
start, as a project near the end, and again in the conclusion. Between
recipes, the reading drags.

I'm not a crafter, though my partner is. I don't know whether someone who
already crafts would weigh the instruction against the ritual the way I did.
