[*Once Upon a Parallel Time*][book] opens with an introduction about Philip
K. Dick rather than by him. It sets out what he wrote, how he built a story,
and which themes he kept returning to, all of it offered as the findings of
research into his style. Nobody signed it. The book credits no editor and no
author for that introduction, and carries no note saying where any of it
came from. Then the stories start, and by then you are holding a checklist to
grade them against.

I spent the rest of the book doing exactly that. Something in the phrasing
sat wrong—not the shape of the stories, which was Dick to the bone, but the
sentences themselves. I kept second-guessing whether I was reading Dick or a
machine's impression of him. I also couldn't stop, because a book that makes
you doubt what's in front of you is close to the most Dick thing that can
happen while reading Dick.

The question turned out to have an answer. Finding it took about ten
minutes. All four stories are his: "Adjustment Team" ran in *Orbit Science
Fiction* in 1954, ["The Defenders"][defenders] in 1953, "Breakfast at
Twilight" in *Amazing Stories* in July 1954, and "The Unreconstructed M" in
*Science Fiction Stories* in January 1957. Three of them sit on [the estate
site's list of Dick stories in the public domain][pd], copyrights nobody
renewed, which lapsed in 1983. The arc reading as classic Dick is not a
forger's skill. It's Dick.

What isn't his is the book. He never assembled these four, never grouped them
by parallel worlds, and never called anything *Once Upon a Parallel Time*.
That title belongs to e-artnow, who published the collection in June 2018.
Ninety-one pages, four public-domain stories, and a frame built around them
by whoever put the file together. Set it beside the *Collected Stories*,
where the same material arrives dated, sourced, and in order. Nothing in this
edition tells you when a word of it was written.

So I wouldn't read this one. The stories are free elsewhere and better
presented. The only thing this edition adds is the part that isn't Dick.

What I checked was the stories, not the text of them. Whether these
particular files were retyped, cleaned up, or run through something on the
way to the store, the book won't say. There's no source note, no edition
history, no editor to ask. That's the real complaint, and it's why knowing
the stories are genuine didn't quite settle the feeling. The suspicion
outlived the answer.
