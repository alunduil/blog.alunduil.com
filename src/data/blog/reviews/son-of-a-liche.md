A year after [*Orconomics*][orconomics], Gorm Ingerson's party are outlaws,
still trying to make amends to the orcs they betrayed. Each of them keeps a
secret from the others. The secrets pull the party apart.
[*Son of a Liche*][liche], the second book of J. Zachary Pike's Dark Profit
Saga, builds from that low point on a rising arc. It's an excellent
continuation of the first book. Its heroes grow in confidence as it goes.
I'll keep to who they become rather than what happens to them.

Gorm carries that arc. In the first book his loyalty to a goblin squire and a
party of misfits was most of what made him lovable. Here he puts that loyalty
to work, bringing each member back to the party in turn. Without him they'd be
a handful of fugitives keeping secrets from each other. With him they become a
group of heroes again, surer of themselves than they ever were in
*Orconomics*.

The pairs inside the party grow too. Jynn, the wizard the title names, hides
who his father is. Laruna, the fire mage, doesn't handle lies at all. Jynn's
secrets cost the two of them what they had. They find their way back to being
friends who help each other. Kaitha, the elf ranger, took the troll Thane for
a supernatural guardian watching over her from the woods. She sets out looking
for him.

The book also sets up the final showdown with the dragon. I read the dragon as
standing, literally and figuratively, for what backs Arth's economy. I don't
know yet where [*Dragonfired*][dragonfired] takes that.
