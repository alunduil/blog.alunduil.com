# Once Upon a Parallel Time (book) — review spine

> **Spoilers.** Minimal. The argument runs on the packaging, not the plots.
> No endings named here or in the post.

**Object:** ebook collection — *Once Upon A Parallel Time: 4 Alternate
Universe Tales in One Edition*, credited to Philip K. Dick, published by
e-artnow, 9 June 2018, ISBN 9788026894216, 91 pages. Read on Google Play
Books; marked Finished in the Media Log 2026-07-12.
**Issue:** #330. **Slug:** `once-upon-a-parallel-time`.
**Title:** Nobody Signed the Introduction.

**Contents, verified.** All four are documented Dick, none written for this
book:

- *Adjustment Team* — *Orbit Science Fiction*, 1954
- *The Defenders* — 1953; [Project Gutenberg #28767][defenders]
- *Breakfast at Twilight* — *Amazing Stories*, July 1954
- *The Unreconstructed M* — *Science Fiction Stories*, January 1957

The first three appear on [the Dick estate site's public-domain list][pd]:
1954–55 copyrights never renewed, expired 1 January 1983. *The
Unreconstructed M* is not on that list. Its status wasn't established here,
so the post doesn't claim it.

**Thesis:** Skip the book, read the stories elsewhere. Dick wrote all four
stories and none of the book — not the title, not the grouping, not the
unsigned introduction that starts the doubt. The suspicion of machine
assembly is well founded and aimed one layer too deep.

**Register constraint:** a book review, not an essay on AI and authorship.
Keep the volume load-bearing. A thousand words on machine-generated
literature with this book as a pretext is a different post.

## Supporting points (thematic, not chronological)

1. **The doubt starts before Dick does.** Evidence: the introduction, a
   framing piece on what Dick wrote and how he built a story, offered as
   research into his style. It primes the reader to grade the prose that
   follows. Nobody signed it — no editor credit, no introduction credit.

2. **The question has a checkable answer.** Evidence: four documented
   magazine publications, 1953–1957, with *The Defenders* on Project
   Gutenberg in full. The arc reading as classic Dick is Dick. The container
   is invented: e-artnow's title, e-artnow's grouping, 2018.

3. **What's skewed is the frame, and the frame is the whole product.**
   Measurement: against the *Collected Stories*, where the same material
   arrives dated, sourced, ordered, and attributed. Evidence: 91 pages, four
   stories bundled by motif, ad copy pitching *Adjustment Team* as "adapted
   into a successful movie featuring Matt Damon." Nothing in the volume
   dates or sources anything. The front matter is the only part being sold
   and the only part that isn't Dick.

4. **The irony is accidental.** An object that makes you doubt what's in
   front of you, with no way to check from inside it, is a Dick premise
   landing on a Dick collection. Commerce produced that, not design. Worth
   one paragraph; it stays out of the thesis.

## Honest tail

I verified the stories, not the text of them. The book carries no source
note, no edition history, and no editor, so whether these files were
retyped, OCR'd, or rewritten can't be settled from inside it. The suspicion
outlives the answer.
