# Once Upon a Parallel Time (book) — review spine

> **Spoilers.** Minimal. The argument runs on the packaging, not the plots,
> and the four stories' premises are already in the publisher's own blurb.
> No endings named here or in the post.

**Object:** ebook collection — *Once Upon A Parallel Time: 4 Alternate
Universe Tales in One Edition*, credited to Philip K. Dick, published by
e-artnow, 9 June 2018, ISBN 9788026894216, 91 pages. Read on Google Play
Books; marked Finished in the Media Log 2026-07-12.
**Issue:** #330. **Slug:** `once-upon-a-parallel-time`.
**Working title:** Nobody Signed the Introduction.

**Contents, verified.** All four are documented Dick, none written for this
book:

- *Adjustment Team* — *Orbit Science Fiction*, 1954
- *The Defenders* — 1953; [Project Gutenberg #28767](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/28767)
- *Breakfast at Twilight* — *Amazing Stories*, July 1954
- *The Unreconstructed M* — *Science Fiction Stories*, January 1957

The first three appear on [the Dick estate site's public-domain list](https://philipdick.com/2012/04/03/public-domain-philip-k-dick-stories/)
— 1954–55 copyrights never renewed, expired 1 January 1983. *The
Unreconstructed M* is not on that list; its status wasn't established here
and the post shouldn't claim it.

**Thesis:** Skip the book, read the stories somewhere else. The question it
provoked — did Dick actually write this? — is the best thing about it, and
it has a checkable answer that splits: he wrote all four stories and none of
the book. Not the title, not the grouping, not the unsigned introduction
that starts the doubt. The suspicion of a machine in the room is well
founded and aimed one layer too deep: what's assembled is the object, not
the prose.

**Register constraint:** a book review, not an essay about AI and
authorship. The volume stays load-bearing. The temptation here is a
thousand words on machine-generated literature with this book as a pretext —
that's a different post. Keep it at what it was like to read this specific
ebook and then go check.

## Supporting points (thematic, not chronological)

1. **The doubt is real, and it starts before Dick does.** Evidence: the
   introduction — a short framing piece on what sort of thing Dick wrote and
   what one of his stories looks like. Reading it is what anchored the
   question and made every phrasing afterwards a piece of evidence. It is
   also the one part of the book nobody put a name to: no editor credit, no
   introduction credit. A book that opens by telling you what Dick sounds
   like, in a voice that declines to identify itself.

2. **The question has an answer, and it took ten minutes to get.** Evidence:
   the four stories are documented magazine publications from 1953–1957,
   with *The Defenders* sitting on Project Gutenberg in full. The arc
   reading as "classic Dick" is not a forger's skill — it is Dick. What is
   invented is the container: the title *Once Upon a Parallel Time* is
   e-artnow's, 2018; the theme that groups the four is e-artnow's; Dick
   never made this book.

3. **What's skewed is the frame, and the frame is the whole product.**
   Measurement: against the *Collected Stories*, where the same material
   arrives dated, sourced, ordered, and attributed. Evidence: 91 pages, four
   stories bundled by motif, sold behind ad copy that pitches *Adjustment
   Team* as "adapted into a successful movie featuring Matt Damon." Nothing
   in the volume tells you when anything was written or where it came from.
   Strip the front matter and you have four PD stories anyone can read free;
   the front matter is the only thing being sold, and it's the part that
   isn't Dick.

4. **The accident is a good one, and it's still an accident.** An object
   that makes you doubt the authenticity of what's in front of you, with no
   way to check from inside it, is a Dick premise landing on a Dick
   collection. Worth saying — it's why the book is worth a paragraph instead
   of nothing. Not worth crediting: nobody built that, commerce did. State
   it flat and don't let it inflate into the thesis.

## Honest tail

The read might be wrong in the direction that can't be closed. I checked the
stories, not the text of them — whether these particular files were
retyped, OCR'd, cleaned up, or passed through something is not something the
book will tell you, because it carries no source note, no edition history,
and no editor to ask. That's the real complaint, and it's why knowing the
stories are genuine doesn't fully settle the feeling. The suspicion outlived
the answer.
