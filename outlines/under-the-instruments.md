# Under the Instruments — outline

*(Reconciled to the shipped post after live drafting. Title puns: the
danger comes in *under* (beneath) the instruments, while the aspiration is
to put the operator *under* (monitored by) them.)*

**Logline.** One week I merged sixty-odd agent-authored PRs across two
repos, and it ran effortlessly — because "slow is smooth, smooth is fast"
names the SRE toil-reduction I practise: the smoothness is the self-
correcting system I built one brick at a time, and the agents' speed is
its payoff. The moment of change: the same system watches the code but has
no sensor pointed at the operator, and the last time I went out it came in
under the instruments — through the part of me that does the noticing,
which is the first to go. Opens at its opposite: inside the effortless
week, before the cost is visible.

**Register.** Hybrid: personal/reflective narrative spine, with the
adage-as-SRE-doctrine re-read as one embedded analytical turn (scene 2).

**Verified citations.**

- "Slow is Smooth, Smooth is Fast" is a section title in *Lights Out,
  Systems On: Validating Instant Power Loss Readiness*, Engineering at
  Meta, 2026-06-03 (linked in body). Quote: *"reliability and velocity are
  two facets of the same coin."* Cited as the doctrine's source; the post
  does not claim employment there.
- PR count, 2026-05-31 to 2026-06-07: ~60 authored by the author (54
  alunduil-chezmoi + 6 alunduil-infrastructure), plus ~38 Renovate.
  Peak day 2026-06-04 (17 merges). Scene 1 anchors on that day.

## 1. The effortless week — *the opposite*

1. Morning of 2026-06-04: branches that went green overnight, cleared
   before work *(five by ~09:00)*
2. The workday: the repositories go quiet for eight hours
3. After dinner, television on: branches open and merge in two-three
   minutes, eight by eight o'clock *(the ease at its hottest)*
4. The ease is earned — the checks do the vetting, I agree with them; this
   is what fast feels like from the inside

## 2. The bricks under the ease — *the lift (analytical turn)*

1. The ease came from the checks, each added on an ordinary evening
   *(observability validation, failure-diff tests, the readability gate)*
2. Build order: deterministic checks first; the judgement brick
   (nondeterministic, good-enough evaluation) still ahead, not yet built
3. The Meta post: reliability and velocity are two facets of the same coin
4. The speed is the payoff of the slow scaffolding — the tension dissolves

## 3. Why it's both my job and my hobby — *the braid*

1. The toil-fixing reflex is career and hobby, the same instinct
2. Spare-time rabbit-holing and yak-shaving, kept to my own time, feeds
   the job instead of eating it
3. The reflex doesn't clock out; most of the time that's a gift — not
   always *(hand-forward to the turn)*

## 4. It's failed me once — *the turn*

1. The one time the drive wasn't a gift, it didn't overheat — it went out
2. It started well before the pandemic: side projects thinned, the urge to
   build for its own sake went quiet so slowly I never caught it leaving
3. The pandemic (a year and a half of working from home) took the last of
   the energy for the job itself; burnout by degrees, not a last straw
4. Losing the wish doesn't feel like a loss; it feels like not today
5. Recovery took years; I still manage depression and anxiety
6. I watch my mood and energy now, but watching isn't reading it right —
   the only gauge I have is never quite calibrated *(no employer named)*

## 5. Under the instruments — *the landing*

1. The reflex can't leave an uncalibrated gauge; the checks watch the code,
   nothing watches the operator
2. So I've thought about instrumenting myself: quantified self, the
   operator as another monitored subsystem *(stated intent)*
3. The gap: a dashboard measures what you think to measure, but what took
   me came in under the instruments, through the faculty that fails first
4. If I ever need the readout to tell me I'm going out, it's already begun
5. Slow is smooth, smooth is fast — and smooth is when I stop being able to
   feel the cost *(admitted gap)*
