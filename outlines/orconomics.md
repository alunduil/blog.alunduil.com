# Orconomics — review spine

> **Spoilers.** Names the book-one twist and Gorm's arc through it.

J. Zachary Pike, *Orconomics* (2014), book one of the Dark Profit Saga.

**Thesis:** Read it, then go straight on to *Son of a Liche*. It's a
well-executed satire of capitalism running amok, and it's worth every word.

## Supporting points (thematic, not chronological)

1. The plot runs on the economics. Evidence: fund executives set up the holy
   quest as a pretext. They wipe out a peaceful orc tribe that started a
   competing business, then use the mess to reclassify peaceful Shadowkin as
   fair game, which restarts the hero market. The heroes become the villains
   in pursuit of profit.
2. Gorm is honour in the gutter. Evidence: he has lost his licence and his
   clan and robs adventurers for a living, yet he sticks up for an
   undocumented goblin. After the betrayal his hope is gone, and he keeps
   going anyway, loyal to his friends.

## Honest tail

I read it as the 2008 housing crisis, and Pike wrote it after following
coverage of the crash
(<https://fantasy-hive.co.uk/2023/09/interview-with-j-zachary-pike-the-dark-profit-saga/>).
Whether it stays true beyond 2008 is open.
